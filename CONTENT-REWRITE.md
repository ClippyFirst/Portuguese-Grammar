# Content rewrite: Portuguese Grammar for Ukrainian learners

## Editorial objective

The grammar reference is designed for Ukrainian-speaking learners who may study Brazilian Portuguese, European Portuguese, or Portuguese used in other Lusophone settings. The content therefore distinguishes:

- grammatical rule vs. usage tendency;
- standard vs. colloquial/register variation;
- PT-BR vs. PT-PT;
- national/regional variation where evidence is available;
- Portuguese grammar vs. Ukrainian translation;
- a productive rule vs. a lexicalised construction.

## Method

Each article should answer, in this order:

1. What is the category or construction?
2. What form does it take?
3. What function does it perform?
4. In which contexts is it preferred?
5. What changes in PT-BR / PT-PT or another documented variety?
6. What will a Ukrainian learner likely transfer incorrectly?
7. What diagnostic question lets the learner choose the form?
8. Which related topics should be read next?

## Pedagogical principles

### Contrastive, not translation-driven

Ukrainian is used to predict interference, not to define Portuguese. A Ukrainian equivalent is presented as a translation, while the Portuguese grammatical explanation remains independent.

### Form-function-context

Tables are not treated as sufficient explanations. Every important paradigm should be connected to examples and to the semantic/pragmatic reason for choosing it.

### Variation-aware teaching

The guide does not collapse Portuguese into one imagined universal norm. PT-BR and PT-PT are treated as major reference varieties. African and other Lusophone varieties are described cautiously and only where there is an adequate descriptive basis.

### Register-aware examples

Examples should be marked when a form is colloquial, formal, literary, regional, or strongly associated with a national variety.

### Error-based learning

Common Ukrainian interference is shown as:
wrong transfer -> natural Portuguese form -> explanation of why the transfer fails.

### Uncertainty discipline

When a phenomenon is gradient, regional, corpus-dependent, or debated, the article must say so instead of converting a tendency into an absolute rule.

## Coverage policy

The repository currently contains a large catalogue of topics plus dedicated content modules. The generated coverage layer has been rewritten so that catalogue topics that do not yet have a dedicated linguistic module are no longer merely placeholder definitions: they receive a consistent learner-oriented explanation, examples, Ukrainian interference guidance, variety awareness, and a diagnostic algorithm.

Dedicated modules remain the preferred place for exhaustive treatment of difficult topics such as:

- articles and reference;
- clitic placement;
- personal infinitive;
- subjunctive and future subjunctive;
- verb tense/aspect contrasts;
- valency and government;
- syntax and information structure;
- PT-BR/PT-PT contrasts;
- regional Portuguese.

## Quality gate

Content changes should be checked with:

- typecheck;
- lint;
- content audit;
- static QA;
- tests;
- production build;
- linguistic source verification for categorical claims.

The Academic Writing Toolkit was also used for a paragraph-level logic check during this rewrite. Its result indicated that short standalone paragraphs should be integrated with neighbouring explanatory prose; this principle is incorporated into the editorial rules above.

## Sources and evidence

The rewrite is informed by comparative and descriptive linguistic sources, including:

- Kato, Mary Aizawa & Ana Maria Martins (2016), “European Portuguese and Brazilian Portuguese: An Overview on Word Order”, in *The Handbook of Portuguese Linguistics*.
- Galves, Charlotte (2026), “Syntax”, in *The Oxford Handbook of the Portuguese Language*.
- Martins, Ana Maria (2026), “Portugal”, in *The Oxford Handbook of the Portuguese Language*.
- Gonçalves, Perpétua (2026), “Mozambique”, in *The Oxford Handbook of the Portuguese Language*.
- Alexandre, Nélia & Rita Gonçalves (2018), “Language contact and variation in Cape Verde and São Tomé and Príncipe”.
- Nascimento et al. (2008), work on comparable corpora of African Portuguese varieties and teaching/learning applications.
- Mendes & Estrela (2008), “Constructions with SE in African varieties of Portuguese”.
- CPLP member-state documentation for the nine Lusophone states.

These sources are used as evidence for variation and grammatical architecture, not as a reason to prescribe one national variety as universally superior.
