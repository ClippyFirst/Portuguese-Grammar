# Canonical Grammar Inventory

**Status:** canonical scope for content production  
**Audience:** Ukrainian L1 speakers learning Portuguese  
**Repository:** `ClippyFirst/Portuguese-Grammar`  
**Date:** 2026-09-25

## 1. Purpose

This document is the **coverage source of truth for content production**.

The repository is not intended to be merely a collection of grammar pages. It is a universal reference for a Ukrainian-speaking learner who needs to:

1. find a Portuguese form;
2. understand what it means and does;
3. understand when it is licensed and when it is not;
4. distinguish it from formally similar constructions;
5. understand what Ukrainian does differently;
6. recognise the construction in real Portuguese;
7. produce it correctly;
8. diagnose and repair a likely learner error;
9. account for relevant variety, register and discourse context.

The inventory therefore covers not only traditional morphology and syntax, but also phonology/orthography, semantics, pragmatics, discourse, lexical grammar and documented variation.

The authoritative descriptive reference used as a scope benchmark is *Gramática do Português*, whose published volumes cover syntax, semantics, morphology, phonology and phonetics and also discuss orthography, discourse, pragmatics and Portuguese varieties. The Camões PLE reference additionally organises communicative-language content across linguistic, pragmatic and notional components and across A1-C2. See the project research notes and sources cited in `docs/EVIDENCE-POLICY.md`.

## 2. What counts as a canonical topic

A **canonical topic** is an independently searchable learner problem or grammatical concept that deserves a stable identity in the knowledge graph.

Not every bullet below must become a standalone page. Some are deliberately **modules** inside a larger article when splitting them would create fragmentation. The distinction is:

- **Article** — independently useful reference unit.
- **Module** — required coverage inside one or more articles.
- **Cross-link** — a concept whose main explanation lives elsewhere but must be discoverable here.

A topic is not considered covered merely because its words appear somewhere in another article.

### Coverage closure rule

A canonical topic is **closed** only when:

- the concept is explicitly named;
- its Portuguese forms/constructions are represented where relevant;
- its function/meaning is described;
- its distribution/constraints are described where relevant;
- Ukrainian contrast is addressed where pedagogically relevant;
- examples exist;
- important variation/register is scoped;
- related topics are linked;
- evidence metadata exists for high-risk claims;
- the topic has a reviewed status.

## 5. Coverage policy for hidden subtopics

A page does **not** close multiple canonical topics merely by mentioning them.

For example:

- a page on `se` does not automatically close passive `se`, impersonal `se`, middle `se` and reciprocal `se`;
- a page on numerals does not close dates, percentages, money, measures and fractional expressions unless those are explicitly covered;
- a page on punctuation does not close punctuation in relative clauses, subordinate clauses and direct speech;
- a page on word formation does not close prefixation, suffixation, conversion, compounding and derivational semantics unless each is explicitly represented;
- a page on questions does not close echo, alternative and negative questions unless they are explicitly represented;
- a page on subordinate clauses does not close conformative, proportional, modal, locative and reduced clauses unless they are explicitly represented.

## 6. Canonical article types

Each inventory item receives one primary article type from `docs/CONTENT-MODEL.md`:

- `concept`
- `form`
- `construction`
- `contrast`
- `usage`
- `reference`
- `error`
- `variation`
- `system`

An item may have secondary modules, but it must have one canonical home.

## 7. Ukrainian-L1 coverage rule

For every substantive article, the editorial workflow must ask:

1. Is there a Ukrainian analogue?
2. If yes, does it function the same way?
3. If no, what Ukrainian structure normally expresses the same meaning?
4. Does Portuguese encode a distinction Ukrainian leaves implicit?
5. Does Ukrainian encode a distinction Portuguese expresses differently?
6. Is there a false formal equivalent?
7. Does a Ukrainian translation hide the Portuguese construction?
8. Is positive transfer plausible?
9. Is a learner-error claim documented, or merely predicted?

The ninth question is critical: the repository must never present an invented statement such as “Ukrainians usually make X error” as an established fact.

## 8. Variation rule

Variation is part of the canonical inventory, but it must not be represented as a simplistic binary:

> Brazil = X; Portugal = Y.

Every variation note should scope, where relevant:

- variety;
- region;
- register;
- spoken/written mode;
- social context;
- standard/normative status;
- frequency;
- evidence.

## 9. Production states

Every inventory item must have one of these states:

- `catalog-only` — canonical topic exists, no article yet;
- `fallback` — generated/placeholder coverage only;
- `review-needed` — substantive content exists but is not closed;
- `reviewed` — passes the content QA contract.

**Fallback never counts as reviewed coverage.**

## 10. Rewrite gate

The repository is ready for mass article rewriting only when:

- every canonical inventory item has a disposition;
- every item is mapped to an existing article, an explicit module, or a new article;
- no unexplained orphan topics remain;
- no duplicate canonical identities remain;
- hidden subtopics have been surfaced;
- high-risk contrastive and variation topics have evidence requirements;
- the renderer can represent the required conditional modules;
- search can find topics by Portuguese form, Ukrainian concept and learner problem;
- the related-topic graph has no broken canonical IDs.

Until then, article rewriting should be treated as **migration work**, not as the final content-production pass.

## 11. What this inventory deliberately does not claim

This inventory is a **coverage ontology**, not a claim that every numbered item must become a separate URL.

It also does not attempt to turn CEFR into the grammar ontology. CEFR is progression metadata. The grammar reference must remain searchable by linguistic phenomenon, communicative meaning and learner problem.

The target is not “the largest possible number of pages”. The target is **closed conceptual coverage with discoverable learner-facing units**.

## 12. Definition of complete coverage

The Portuguese grammar reference is considered **coverage-complete** when a Ukrainian-speaking learner can reasonably search for any central Portuguese grammatical, phonological, orthographic, semantic, pragmatic, discourse or lexical-grammatical phenomenon and receive either:

1. a dedicated canonical article;
2. a clearly identified module in a canonical article; or
3. a typed redirect to the canonical article that owns the concept.
